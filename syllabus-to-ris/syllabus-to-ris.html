Tips for the best results
  • Sections: put each heading on its own line, e.g. “Week 2: Social capital”, “Session 3”, “Section: Theories of capital” or “## Global goals”.
  • Importance for one reading: start it with “Required:”, “Recommended:” or “Optional:”, or add [required], [recommended] or [optional] anywhere in it.
  • Importance for a group: put “Required readings”, “Recommended readings” or “Further reading” on its own line. It applies to the readings below it, up to the next heading.
  • Include a DOI or web link when you have one. It is used as given.
  • Notes in brackets, such as “(PDF in Canvas)” or “(25 pages)”, are kept as notes for students.
  • Any common citation style works (APA, Harvard, Chicago). Each reading should start with the author’s name.
  • You can paste the whole syllabus. Lines that aren’t references are skipped, and you can remove anything left over in the next step.

Download & import

Import settings

These are VU Reading Lists’ exact labels (Required reading, Recommended reading, Optional reading). They must match exactly, so only change them if VU changes its wording. When importance isn’t set this way, the level is added to the note instead.

Import into VU Reading Lists

  1. Sign in to VU Reading Lists and open My Bookmarks. Choose Add → Import citations and select the .ris file. You can import straight into a new list, or into My Bookmarks and add the readings to an existing list later.
  2. Import straight into a new list: the readings keep their order, and each week heading appears as a paragraph above its readings.
  3. The import can’t create real sections. To add them, use Add section at each heading paragraph and move that week’s readings into it.
  4. Readings with a DOI or ISBN are matched to the library’s holdings. Their details may be updated from the catalogue after import.
  5. For readings marked “PDF in Canvas”, upload the file or request a digitisation from the list item. A file can hold up to 300 readings.

      README — Syllabus to RIS
      
    

    



What this does


Turns a course syllabus into a RIS file for VU Reading Lists. It splits the syllabus into separate readings, keeps the week headings as sections, finds a DOI or ISBN for each reading where one exists, and lets you check everything before you download the file.



Basic steps


    

  1. Paste the reading list (or the whole syllabus), or upload a PDF, Word (.docx) or text file.
    2. 

  2. Fill in your email address. Crossref and OpenAlex answer faster and more reliably when it's included.
    3. 

  3. Click Find readings and wait for the searches to finish (about 1–3 minutes for 20–30 readings).
    4. 

  4. Check the readings marked amber. Accept or reject suggested matches, or paste a DOI or ISBN under Edit.
    5. 

  5. Download the .ris file for the whole list, or one file per week.
    6. 




What the colours mean


    

  • Green (DOI / ISBN): identified, so the library can link the reading to its holdings.
    • 

  • Blue (Web link): imports as a web page or report.
    • 

  • Amber (Needs a look): no identifier found, or an uncertain match (for example a different edition) for you to accept or reject.
    • 




Marking sections and importance in your syllabus


VU Reading Lists has three importance levels: Required reading, Recommended reading and Optional reading. The tool reads them from the syllabus in any of these ways:


    

  • One reading: start it with a label, e.g. Required: Putnam, R. D. (1995)…, Recommended: …, Optional: … or Further reading: ….
    • 

  • One reading, label at the end: add a tag in brackets anywhere in it, e.g. … Verso. [optional] or (recommended).
    • 

  • A group of readings: put a heading on its own line, e.g. Required readings, Recommended reading, Further reading or Optional literature. It applies to the readings below it until the next section or group heading.
    • 

  • Other words: Essential, Core, Compulsory and Mandatory count as required; Suggested as recommended; Further, Additional, Background and Extra reading as optional. Dutch also works: Verplicht, Aanbevolen, Optioneel.
    • 

  • Unlabelled readings: in the check step, use Set for all unmarked to give every reading without importance the same level in one click, for example Required reading. You can still change single readings with the menu on their card.
    • 



Sections come from headings on their own line:


    

  • Numbered sessions: Week 2: Social capital, Session 3, Lecture 4, Seminar 1, College 5.
    • 

  • Themes: Section: Theories of capital, Theme: …, or a Markdown heading such as ## Global goals.
    • 



Example (click Load example above the text box to try it):


Week 1: Introduction
Required: Putnam, R. D. (1995). Bowling alone… Journal of Democracy, 6(1), 65–78.
Recommended: Granovetter, M. S. (1973). The strength of weak ties…
Optional: Anderson, B. (2006). Imagined Communities… Verso.

Week 2: Forms of capital
Required readings
Bourdieu, P. (1986). The forms of capital. In J. Richardson (Ed.)…
Coleman, J. S. (1988). Social capital in the creation of human capital…
Further reading
Lin, N. (2001). Social Capital… Cambridge University Press.

Week 3: Global goals
United Nations (2015). Transforming our world… https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda [recommended]


Result: Putnam, Bourdieu and Coleman are Required reading; Granovetter and the United Nations report are Recommended reading; Anderson and Lin are Optional reading. Each lands in its week.


VU Reading Lists can't import real sections. The tool keeps them in three ways: each section heading is added as a paragraph above its readings, each reading's note starts with its section, and you can download one file per section.


You can change the importance of any reading in the check step, using the menu on its card.



Other tips for the best results


    

  • Include a DOI or web link when you have one; it's used as given. Library proxy links are converted to normal doi.org links.
    • 

  • Notes in brackets, such as (PDF in Canvas) or (25 pages), are kept as notes for students.
    • 

  • Any common citation style works (APA, Harvard, Chicago). Each reading should start with the author's name.
    •

How links to the full text are found


    

  • Readings with a DOI or ISBN link to the library's holdings. With your email address filled in, the tool also asks Unpaywall for a free, legal full-text copy and adds it as a second link.
    • 

  • Readings without a DOI or ISBN (working papers, reports, policy briefs) are looked up in OpenAlex. The tool then picks, in this order:
  
      
  
    1. an open full-text copy;
      2. 
  
    2. a copy of the original PDF saved by the Internet Archive, when the original address may no longer work;
      3. 
  
    3. a PDF link it couldn't check;
      4. 
  
    4. a record page, which may not include the full text.
      5. 
  
    • 

  • Each reading shows which kind of link it has. For a PDF link or record page, a Replace with a better link field appears so you can paste a better one.
    • 

  • Google Scholar can't be searched automatically. If the tool finds nothing, the reading gets a Google Scholar search link for its exact title and first author, so students land on the search results. You can turn this off under Import settings, or paste a direct link instead, which is better for students.
    • 




Good to know


    

  • OpenAlex shares one small free daily allowance between everyone on the same network, such as the VU network. If its searches start failing, add a free personal key under OpenAlex API key. Google Books also has a shared daily limit; when it's reached, the tool uses Open Library instead.
    • 

  • The free lookup services limit how many searches they accept per minute, so searches are paced. If a reading shows "Couldn't reach …", wait a minute and click Search again for amber readings.
    • 

  • ISBNs from Google Books or Open Library are the best match for the year in the syllabus. Check it's the edition students should use.
    • 

  • Working papers, reports, policy briefs and dissertation summaries often have no DOI or ISBN. The tool then looks them up in OpenAlex, which lists many of them with a web link, and adds that link. Google Scholar can't be searched automatically, so if OpenAlex doesn't have it, you can copy a link from Google Scholar into the Paste a link, DOI or ISBN field on an amber reading. With a link, the reading imports as a report or web page.
    • 




Privacy


The tool runs in your browser and your syllabus file is never uploaded. It needs an internet connection. To find identifiers and full-text links, it sends the title, authors and DOI of each reading to Crossref, OpenAlex, Google Books, Open Library, Unpaywall and the Internet Archive. If you fill in your email address, it goes to Crossref, OpenAlex and Unpaywall. Fonts and the PDF and Word readers are loaded from Google Fonts and cdnjs. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere else.
